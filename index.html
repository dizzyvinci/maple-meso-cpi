    What inflation.watch measures

    Mesos have no official price. The nearest thing is what they buy. This site keeps two gauges for the MapleStory Classic World Free Market:

    • Meso CPI: the cost, in mesos, of a fixed basket of the most-traded items, compared with what the same basket cost in launch week. Above 100 means mesos buy less than they did at launch; below 100, more.
    • NX to meso: how many mesos 1,000 NX is worth, read off Cash Shop items that players buy with NX and resell for mesos. When the number rises, each NX buys more mesos, which is the same thing as mesos getting cheaper.

    The basket

    The headline basket is the 50 items where the most mesos change hands: every item is ranked by its launch-week meso volume, average daily sales during Oct 21–27 times the average daily sale price that week, and the top 50 are taken, provided the item sold at least 50 times a day (the liquidity floor that keeps a rare item with one huge sale out). Cash Shop items are left out (they belong to the NX gauge), as are the Aurora Stamp palette coupons and items with a flagged regime change (asterisked below). The same volume share is the item's weight, capped at 8% so one scroll cannot move the index on its own; excess weight is spread over the other items. This is how the other trackers rank "volume" too: sales times price, not sales alone, so a 2-million-meso scroll that sells 60 times a day outweighs an ore that sells 30,000 times for 300.

    The five category gauges use the same recipe on the 12 most-traded items of each category, with a 20% cap per item. The basket is reviewed, not rebuilt, so day-to-day moves come from prices rather than from items entering and leaving.

    Why these are sales, not reports

    Every price here is a trade that happened, read straight off the game. A Free Market shop lists each item on a row with its price; when someone buys, the game greys that row out (the stock is gone), and when the shop closes the sold rows have vanished. The scanners behind both feeds watch the shop windows on every channel and log a sale the moment a row turns grey or disappears, at the price that was printed on it. Nobody types anything in. That is different in kind from the other ways prices get collected: a smega is a seller announcing an asking price, which says what they hope for, not what anyone paid; a player report on a site like NiaDB is what one person says they saw or sold; a "listed now" figure is also an ask. Asks sit above sales, which is why the NiaDB cross-check runs high. The medians used here are of greyed-out rows only, and the daily feed keeps the asking prices in a separate column that the index never touches.

    The basket in one paragraph, next to the US CPI

    The Meso CPI basket is the 50 items on the Classic World Free Market where the most mesos change hands, chosen and weighted by launch-week meso volume (daily sales times sale price), with a liquidity floor of 50 sales a day so a rarity with one huge sale cannot enter. Weighting by meso volume rather than by count means the basket reflects where mesos actually go: capped at 8% so no single scroll steers the index, it comes to roughly two thirds scrolls, with weapons and equipment most of the rest, and only a few percent in ores, drops and potions, because those trade in huge numbers for a few hundred mesos each. The index is a fixed-basket Laspeyres: the weighted average of each item's price relative to its launch-week price, with launch week set to 100. The US CPI from the Bureau of Labor Statistics works the same way at the top level, a fixed basket priced against a base period with expenditure weights, and differs everywhere else: BLS weights come from household expenditure surveys over about 200 item categories and are updated yearly, its prices are listed prices sampled from shops once a month, lower-level aggregation uses geometric means to absorb substitution within a category, quality changes get hedonic adjustment, and seasonal adjustment is published separately. This index uses actual sale medians from shop scans, recomputes daily with a live layer every ten minutes, treats every item as its own category with no substitution or quality adjustment, has no seasonal adjustment, and sits on a one-week base rather than the 1982–84 three-year base. It is closer in spirit to a commodity price index than to a cost-of-living measure, and like the US headline figure it is dominated by one group: shelter is about a third of the CPI, scrolls are two thirds of this one, which is why the category gauges exist.

    What is in the basket

    Every item in the headline basket, grouped the way the category gauges group them, with its weight and latest price. Icons are the game's own item art, served by the maplestory.io community API that the other trackers also use; where the API has no sprite for an item, or a host blocks remote images, the site draws its own stand-in (scrolls banded by tier, with a dot for the stat; ores, gems and potions in their own colours).

    The arithmetic

    CPIt = 100 × Σ wi × ( pi,t / pi,base )
    • pi,t is the item's daily median sale price. A day counts only with five or more sales (the feed's own low-data line); otherwise the last counted price is carried forward for up to three days, after which the item sits out until it trades again and the remaining weights are rescaled.
    • pi,base is the mean of its daily medians over launch week, so the launch-week average is exactly 100.
    • Contributions on the Index page are wi × (pi,t/pi,base − 1), in index points.

    NX to meso

    For each day, every sale of a qualifying Cash Shop item is added up in mesos and in the NX Nexon charges for it. The rate is the ratio: total mesos paid divided by total NX those items cost. Items qualify when they have a fixed NX price and the buyer gets the same thing either way: inventory and storage slot coupons, AP and SP Reset Scrolls, pets and Water of Life, pet skills, pet equipment, and hair, face and color coupons. Mystery Fashion Crates are left out (their resale value depends on luck), as are megaphones and charms sold in bundles.

    ItemNX

    NX prices come from Nexon's Founder's Access Cash Shop notice (October 5, 2026), which still applies after Grand Launch. In Nexon's store 1,000 NX costs about one US dollar, which is why the Index page also shows what a million mesos is worth in NX. Mesos cannot be bought or sold for money; the rate here is the Cash Shop exchange, not a cash price.

    Three NX rates, three kinds of evidence

    The chart on the Index tab can carry three NX rates, and they are not supposed to agree. Sales (gold and blue): what buyers paid, from greyed-out shop rows. Reported (pink): NiaDB's indicator, built from prices signed-in players post on NiaDB over the last 24 hours, recorded here once per build with credit; it mixes asks and reported sales, so it runs above the sale rate. Smega asks (teal): what sellers shout in megaphones. The figures come from Kerning Exchange, which reads every smega in game and keeps, per item, the 7-day median of selling shouts, the 7-day median of buying shouts and how many players shouted each. This site reads those item pages for the same cash items as the NX gauge, every two hours, and computes a shout-weighted rate: the sum of selling medians over the sum of NX prices, each item weighted by the number of players who shouted it; the bid rate is the same with buying medians. A seller's shout is an ask and a buyer's shout is a bid, so the two bracket the sale rate; the gap between them is the spread the market is haggling over. Both are 7-day medians, so they move slowly by construction. The site also accepts a local smega.log (one line per message, time then text) and computes a six-hour ask rate from it; that path only runs when the file exists.

    Where the numbers come from

    Two layers. The daily series (one close per day since Founder's Access) comes from the developer feed maplestory-classic.com publishes for its Free Market tracker (market.json): daily median sale prices from shop scans, refreshed once or twice a day. The live layer comes from mesos.watch, whose in-game scanners read every Free Market shop on every channel and log sales as they happen; its typical price for an item is the median of its last thousand sales, with the hours they took. inflation.watch polls those medians for the basket and the cash items every ten minutes, with mesos.watch's permission, and recomputes the live NX rate (weighted by each item's sales per hour) and a live CPI estimate against the same launch-week base. The gold dots on the charts are those polls; the blue line is the daily close.

    Why not faster than ten minutes: the figures are medians over hours of sales, so they barely move minute to minute, and polling a fan site harder than that only costs it bandwidth. Ten minutes keeps the page within one poll of the scanners.

    How NiaMeowDB's indicator differs. NiaDB's NX:Meso Indicator is built from prices that signed-in players report on NiaDB (their own listings, screenshots they import, and listings marked sold), over the last 24 hours, for Cash Shop items with a fixed NX price. It is not read from smegas or from the game. Because asking prices sit above sale prices, it tends to run higher than a scanner-based sale median; it is shown here as a credited cross-check, which is what NiaDB's reuse policy allows.

    Updating, and which sites allow it

    The page is a static build. build.py pulls both layers, recomputes every number above and writes index.html and data.json; run it every ten minutes (one cron line or the included GitHub Actions workflow) and an open page reloads itself when a newer build lands. mesos.watch's public rules say its data serves the site only, so the live layer runs with its permission and identifies itself in every request; meso.watch and maplestory.watch forbid automated collection and are not used; NiaMeowDB allows the credited figure shown; kerning.gg (Kerning Exchange) reads every smega in game and publishes per-item medians of what sellers and buyers shout, its robots.txt allows crawling and it states no restriction on reuse, so the smega series reads one page per NX item every two hours with an identified user agent and credits it; maplestory.market publishes no terms we could find. Also checked and set aside: freemarketclassic.com (smegas and an in-game FM tracker, terms unpublished) and meso.watch's and maplestory.watch's own smega pages, which fall under their no-automation rules.

    Reading it honestly

    • Medians summarise a day of trades; thin days (early Founder's Access, the first hours of a partial day) are noisier.
    • Fixed baskets drift from what people actually buy as the server ages; the basket should be reviewed when the level cap or content changes.
    • Materials falling while scrolls rise is a change in relative prices, not just a change in the value of mesos; the category gauges exist to show that.
